## What it is

Three tools that eat the paperwork around teaching:

1. **Lesson plans** — standards-aligned, with a timed sequence and differentiation
2. **Quizzes** — four formats, with the answer key on its own page
3. **Feedback** — rubric-based, quoting the student's own words

Every result downloads as .docx, prints letter-size, or copies as Markdown for Google Docs and your LMS.

The gap it fills: teachers do not lack AI that can write a lesson plan. They lack AI that cites the standard, allocates the minutes, and puts the answer key on a separate page so it can be handed out.

---

## What it does well

- **Cites the anchor standard** — you pick the framework, the standard drives the objectives and the assessment
- **Timed sequence included** — objectives, timings, differentiation, materials and an exit ticket, not a vague paragraph
- **Four quiz formats** — multiple choice, short answer, true/false, fill-in-the-blank
- **Separate answer key** — on its own page, printable without edits
- **Feedback quotes the student** — names what worked and says exactly what to fix next, at class scale rather than essay scale
- **Eight alignment frameworks** — Common Core, NGSS, TEKS, Florida BEST, UK, Australian, IB, AP
- **Student work is not stored** — responses are sent to the model, graded, and discarded; nothing is used for training
- **Real export paths** — .docx, letter-size print, or Markdown for Google Docs and your LMS

## Where it falls short

- **The free tier has a monthly cap.** If you are planning five classes a day, you will hit it. Budget for the $12 rather than fighting the cap.
- **"Standards-aligned" means the framework is respected**, not that your specific district's pacing guide is. Read before it reaches a classroom.
- **Feedback quality scales with your rubric.** Paste a vague rubric, get vague feedback that quotes the student nicely.
- **Not a gradebook or an LMS.** It produces documents; it does not track a class over a term.

## FAQ

**Do the lesson plans cite real standards?**
You pick the framework — Common Core, NGSS, TEKS, Florida BEST, UK, Australian, IB or AP — and the anchor standard drives the objectives and the assessment.

**Is student work stored?**
No. Responses pasted into the grading tool are sent to the model, graded and discarded; nothing is used for training.

**Can I edit the output?**
Yes. Everything exports to .docx, prints letter-size, or copies as Markdown for Google Docs and your LMS.

**How much does it cost?**
Free to start with no credit card; $12 a month or $84 a year if you hit the free cap.

**Will it match my district's pacing guide?**
It matches the framework you select, not your district's specific sequence. Treat the output as a strong first draft and check the order against your own scope and sequence.
