## What it is

A guide and workspace for personal AI: turn your own documents into a private index that answers with citations, instead of pasting context into a chat box every morning.

It covers the four things that actually decide whether a personal AI is worth keeping — **privacy, memory, ingestion and latency** — and compares the main apps without vendor copy.

The gap it fills: generic chatbots have no memory of your files and no obligation to keep them private. A real personal AI is an index of what *you* wrote, answering from that store, with the passage attached so you can check it.

---

## What it does well

- **Documents stay in your namespace** — no training on your data
- **Every answer cites the passage** in your own file, so you can verify instead of trusting
- **Deleting a document takes its vectors with it** — deletion means deletion, not just hiding the file
- **Practical ingestion** — PDF, DOCX, Markdown and CSV go in as they are; reformatting before upload is the fastest way to never upload
- **Exports and one-action delete** are treated as requirements, not premium features
- **Independent comparison tables** plus a published how-we-test page, so the methodology is inspectable

## The four questions that decide if it is worth keeping

This is the framing the guide uses, and it is the right one. Ask any vendor these before you upload anything:

| Question | Why it decides everything |
|---|---|
| **Privacy** — is my content used for training? | If yes, your notes are someone else's training data forever |
| **Memory** — is this a permanent index or a chat session? | A chat window you have to re-feed is not a personal AI |
| **Ingestion** — what happens when I upload a 200-page PDF? | If it needs reformatting first, you will stop using it in a week |
| **Latency** — how long until an answer with a citation? | Slow enough and you go back to grepping filenames |

## Who it is for

- Asking your own research notes a question months later
- Recalling what you agreed with a client, with the source email attached
- Turning a pile of PDFs into something you can interrogate
- Keeping a private decision log that stays searchable

Not for: people whose notes live happily in one app and who never need to ask them anything. That is a notes problem, not a personal-AI problem.

## Where it falls short

- **It is a guide *and* a workspace**, which makes the entry point ambiguous. If you want a tool, skim past the guide; if you want to choose a tool, the guide is the point.
- **Not a replacement for the notes app you already live in.** If your notes are in Notion, the switching cost is real. If they are scattered PDFs, that is exactly the case it solves.
- **Citation-first is slower than a fluent guess.** You will sometimes get "I could not find this in your documents" where a chatbot would have confidently invented something. That is the trade being made, deliberately.

## FAQ

**What is a personal AI, really?**
A private index of everything you have written down — notes, PDFs, research — that answers from that store with citations, instead of from the open web.

**Is my content used for training?**
On Musebrain's workspace, no: documents stay in your namespace. The guide also tells you which questions to ask any vendor before trusting them.

**Can I export or delete everything?**
Yes — export and one-action delete are treated as requirements, not features. Deleting a document removes its vectors too.

**What can I upload?**
PDF, DOCX, Markdown and CSV as they are; reformatting before upload is the fastest way to stop uploading.
